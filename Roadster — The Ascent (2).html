STUNT 01

Up the wall

The Roadster drove up a Hot Wheels–style track, went fully vertical, then rolled over onto its roof.

STUNT 02

Stuck to the ceiling

Tesla says a multi-mode fan and ground-sealing system pulls air from under the car, making downforce strong enough to hold it upside down, even stationary.

STUNT 03

The kickflip

Using SpaceX cold-gas thrusters (stored high-pressure gas pushed out of nozzles), it cleared a row of Tesla vehicles — Model 3, Model Y, Cybercab, Cybertruck and Semi — spinning mid-air.

REALITY CHECK

Attendees filmed it too, so it happened. But the stunt car had no one driving it, and Tesla hasn’t shown that customer cars can do this.

STUNT 04

0–60 in ~1 second

A recorded run with rear thrusters. Reports range from 0.81 to 1.0 seconds, and Tesla hasn’t confirmed a production spec. The 2017 promise was 1.9 s.

FINE PRINT

Electrek noted the thrusters appeared to fire before the car began moving, so treat it as a demo, not a road test.

ROAD READY

Performance

$200K base, 2 seats. Active aero and underbody suction fan. 0–60 in under 1.6 s, 210+ mph, 2G cornering.

RACE READY

Thrust

Street-legal, 2 seats. 200 L trunk tank feeds 4 rear cold-gas thrusters. 0–60 in under 0.9 s.

AIR READY

Rocket

1 seat, experimental track car. 600 L air tank, 10 thrusters. 0–60 in 0.59 s, plus jumps, hover and mid-air spins.
